## Summary

Introduced three types in `platform.messaging.retry` that complete the delay-calculation half of the retry model introduced in ARB-022.6.1. `BackoffStrategy` declares the delay contract; `ExponentialBackoffStrategy` computes delays using `initialDelay × multiplierᵃᵗᵗᵉᵐᵖᵗ⁻²` capped at `maxDelay`; `BackoffDelay` is an immutable `Duration` wrapper consumed by the future scheduler. The dispatcher (`DispatchOutboxMessageService`) is untouched — scheduling is deferred to ARB-022.6.3.

## Architectural Decisions

### 1. `BackoffStrategy` is independent of `RetryPolicy`

The two interfaces have no compile-time relationship. `RetryPolicy.evaluate` answers *should we retry?*; `BackoffStrategy.nextDelay` answers *how long should we wait before the next attempt?*. The ARB-022.6.3 scheduler will compose both to drive the actual retry loop, but neither depends on the other today. This matches the layered diagram in the task specification.

### 2. Delay formula: `initialDelay × multiplierᵃᵗᵗᵉᵐᵖᵗ⁻²`, capped

`attempt=1` is the first try — no prior failure, so the delay is always `BackoffDelay.ZERO`. `attempt=2` is the first retry and receives `initialDelay × multiplier⁰ = initialDelay`. Each subsequent attempt multiplies by `multiplier`. The exponent `attempt-2` produces the standard geometric progression starting at the first retry.

### 3. `BackoffDelay` wraps `java.time.Duration`, not raw milliseconds

The `Duration` type is precise and self-documenting. The scheduler can consume milliseconds via `value().toMillis()` when needed; the strategy itself stays at the logical level. `BackoffDelay.ZERO` is a pre-constructed sentinel to avoid allocating `Duration.ZERO` wrappers on every first-attempt call.

### 4. `multiplier` must be strictly greater than 1.0

A multiplier of exactly 1.0 produces flat (non-exponential) delays; a value below 1.0 shrinks delays. Neither is useful for outbox backoff. The constructor rejects `≤ 1.0` with a clear message. Jitter and randomization are explicitly deferred.

### 5. `maxDelay >= initialDelay` is enforced at construction

Accepting `maxDelay < initialDelay` would mean the cap is always hit on the first retry and every attempt after that — a configuration that signals a mistake. Failing fast at construction is safer than silently returning `maxDelay` as the first delay.

### 6. `ExponentialBackoffStrategy` is `final`

It is a leaf implementation, not designed for subclassing. `final` makes the class ineligible for CGLIB proxying (which is correct — it is never a Spring bean).

## Implementation Notes

`Math.round(initialMs * Math.pow(multiplier, attempt - 2))` is used for the delay calculation. For the values expected in practice (initialDelay in the hundreds-of-milliseconds range, multiplier ≈ 2, attempts < 30), floating-point precision is adequate — the rounding error is at most 1 ms. If sub-millisecond precision were required, `BigDecimal` would be appropriate; it is not warranted here.

## Tradeoffs

- No jitter is implemented in this slice. Jitter (randomizing delays to avoid thundering-herd retries from multiple workers) is a known omission noted in the out-of-scope list. The `BackoffStrategy` interface accommodates a jitter-aware implementation in a future task.
- The integer-millisecond resolution of `Duration.ofMillis` is sufficient for the outbox retry use case. No sub-millisecond precision is needed.
